
# Terminologies & Rational

## Standards

 - ISSB (International Sustainability Standards Board): Goal is to develop IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards to provide investors with information about companies’ sustainability risks and opportunities.

 - GRI (Global Reporting Initiative): GRI provides a universal sustainability reporting framework emphasizing transparency on an organization’s economic, environmental, and social impacts. It guides leadership to identify material topics and engage stakeholders meaningfully. The benefit lies in improved accountability, reputation, and stakeholder trust through standardized, comparable sustainability disclosures.

 - SASB (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board): SASB focuses on financially material sustainability information relevant to investors, tailored by industry. It enables senior leaders to link ESG factors directly with financial performance. Organizations benefit through better capital access, investor confidence, and clear integration of sustainability risks into strategic and financial decision-making.

 - CSRD (Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive): A European Union mandate, CSRD standardizes ESG reporting for corporate transparency and comparability. It drives leadership toward measurable sustainability outcomes aligned with EU taxonomy. Benefits include compliance readiness, enhanced market credibility, and improved risk management across environmental, social, and governance dimensions.

 - TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures): TCFD focuses on climate-related risks and opportunities under governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics. It pushes leaders to integrate climate resilience into corporate planning. The organization benefits from stronger investor assurance, risk foresight, and adaptive strategies against climate-driven financial impacts.

 - IFRS Sustainability & Climate Standards (ISSB): Issued by the International Sustainability Standards Board, IFRS S1 and S2 unify sustainability and climate disclosures globally. Senior leaders gain a clear, investor-focused framework aligning ESG data with financial reporting. Organizations benefit through global comparability, reduced reporting fragmentation, and stronger alignment between sustainability performance and enterprise value.

 - ISO 14067 is an international standard that provides requirements and guidelines for quantifying the carbon footprint of a product (CFP) throughout its entire life cycle. It establishes a standardized methodology for calculating greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from raw material extraction to end-of-life disposal, enabling businesses to accurately measure, report, and communicate their product's climate impact.

 - ISO 14040 and 14044 are international standards for conducting a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA). ISO 14040 provides the fundamental principles and framework for LCA, while ISO 14044 specifies the detailed requirements and provides guidelines for executing an LCA study. Together, they form the basis for assessing a product's environmental impact from "cradle to grave".

 - The GHG (GreenHouse Gas Protocol is the world's most widely used set of accounting standards for measuring and reporting greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, developed by the World Resources Institute (WRI) and the World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD). It provides a global framework for companies, governments, and organizations to consistently quantify and manage their emissions to inform climate action and meet climate goals. Its standards are used in almost every major greenhouse gas accounting and reporting program worldwide

 - STBi refers to the Science Based Targets initiative, which helps companies set greenhouse gas emission reduction targets in line with the latest climate science to limit global warming to below \(1.5^{\circ }C\). This provides a credible pathway to achieving net-zero emissions, enhances investor confidence, reduces risk, and can improve profitability for businesses.

### Summary Insight:

 - BRSR aligns conceptually with GRI and CSRD (broad ESG and double materiality).

 - SASB, TCFD, and IFRS S1/S2 are investor-focused and financially material subsets.

 - IFRS (ISSB) acts as a global convergence layer, integrating SASB and TCFD principles and bridging them with broader sustainability disclosures like BRSR/GRI/CSRD.
